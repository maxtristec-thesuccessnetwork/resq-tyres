The real cost is the standing still, not the tyre

For a working vehicle, the tyre itself is rarely the expensive part. The expensive part is the drops that do not get made, the job that gets rescheduled, the customer who is told you are not coming, and the hourly cost of a driver sitting on a hard shoulder waiting for someone to arrive.

That is why we come to the vehicle rather than asking it to come to us. A van loaded with tools, stock or a part-finished delivery round should not be limping to a depot or sitting on a trailer. We are typically with a customer in 15 to 30 minutes, and the work is done at the roadside, in the yard or on the drive, with the load left where it is.

Fitted overnight so the vehicle starts the morning ready

Most commercial vehicles are only free when nobody is using them. Booking a tyre change into a working day means losing that day twice, once for the fault and once for the fix.

We work 24/7, so the practical option for many businesses is to have the tyre fitted after the round finishes or before it starts. If a driver notices a sidewall split or a slow puncture at the end of a shift, we can fit that night while the van is parked, and the vehicle goes out in the morning without anyone losing an hour.

The same applies to planned work. If you already know a tyre is close to the legal limit, planned fitting is available Monday to Friday, and it can be arranged for a time that does not sit in the middle of your busiest hours.

Van tyres are not car tyres in the same size

This matters more than most drivers realise. A van tyre may look identical to a car tyre of the same size, but it is built differently, with a stronger casing designed to carry a much heavier load at pressure. Commercial tyres are normally marked as reinforced or carry a commercial marking, and every tyre has a load rating and a speed rating that must at least meet what the vehicle manufacturer specifies.

Fitting a standard car tyre to a laden van because the size on the sidewall matches is not a saving. It risks premature failure, uneven wear, poor handling under load, and problems with insurance and roadworthiness. We check what the vehicle actually calls for and fit to that specification, including on light commercials, crew cabs, tippers and taxis that spend their lives fully loaded.

Common questions

Can you fit van tyres overnight so the vehicle is ready in the morning?

Yes. We operate 24 hours a day, 365 days a year, so tyres can be fitted after a shift finishes or before the round starts. The vehicle stays where it is parked, whether that is your depot, a yard or the driver's home address, and goes out in the morning without losing working time.

Are van tyres different from car tyres of the same size?

Yes. Van and light commercial tyres are built with stronger construction to carry heavier loads, and they carry load and speed ratings that must meet what the vehicle manufacturer specifies. A car tyre in the same size is not a like-for-like replacement, even though it looks similar on the sidewall.

Do you cover more than one vehicle for a business?

We can attend multiple vehicles at the same site, and we work with couriers, tradespeople, taxi firms and small fleets across Leeds, Bradford and Wakefield. Arrangements depend on what you run and where it is based, so the simplest thing is to call 07438 562633 and talk it through.

What if the locking wheel nut key is missing?

It happens often on vehicles that have changed drivers. We carry specialist locking wheel nut removal tools, so a missing or damaged key does not stop the job. We can remove the nut and complete the tyre change on site rather than sending the vehicle away to be dealt with elsewhere.
