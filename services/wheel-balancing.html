What wheel balancing actually is

A wheel and tyre assembly is never perfectly even in weight. Small differences in the casting of the wheel, the construction of the tyre and even the position of the valve mean that one part of the assembly is slightly heavier than the rest. Once the wheel is spinning at road speed, that small imbalance is thrown outwards on every rotation, and the assembly begins to shake rather than roll smoothly.

Balancing corrects this. The wheel is spun on a balancer, which identifies where the heavy point sits and how much correction is needed, and small counterweights are fitted to the rim to even the distribution out. There are two elements to it — the side-to-side distribution and the distribution across the width of the wheel — and a proper balance addresses both.

The symptoms, and what they tell you

An out-of-balance wheel produces a vibration that comes and goes with speed rather than being constant. It usually appears within a particular band of motorway speeds, worsens as you accelerate into that band, and often eases off again as you go faster still. That speed-dependent character is the clearest sign that you are dealing with balance rather than something structural.

Where you feel it matters just as much as when.

  • A vibration through the steering wheel usually points to a front wheel.
  • A vibration felt through the seat, the floor or the body of the car usually points to a rear wheel.

Rear imbalance is the one people live with longest, because it is vaguer and easier to blame on the road surface. Left alone, either kind puts avoidable load on your tyres, wheel bearings and suspension, and wears the tyre unevenly.

Why wheels lose their balance

Weights do not stay put forever. The most common cause is a pothole or a sharp kerb strike, which can knock a weight clean off the rim. A wheel that was fine on Monday can be noticeably rough by Friday for exactly that reason.

Balance also drifts as the tyre wears, because rubber is not removed evenly around the circumference. A puncture repair, a wheel refurbishment, or anything else that alters the assembly will change the balance too. And every newly fitted tyre needs balancing from scratch: the old weights were matched to the old tyre and mean nothing once it has been replaced. That is why balancing is part of every tyre fit we carry out, not an extra bolted on afterwards.

When a vibration is not a balancing problem

Not every vibration is a balance problem, and we would rather tell you that on the driveway than take money for a job that will not fix it. A few things get confused with it regularly.

  • Wheel alignment, or tracking. Misalignment pulls the car to one side, wears tyres unevenly across the tread and leaves the steering wheel sitting off-centre. It does not usually cause a rhythmic vibration at speed.
  • A buckled or cracked wheel. A wheel bent out of true will often shake across a wide range of speeds and cannot be corrected with weights. A balancer shows this up quickly.
  • Worn suspension or steering components. Tired bushes, ball joints, top mounts or a failing wheel bearing tend to produce knocking, a droning noise, or a wander that changes over bumps and through corners rather than purely with speed.
  • Warped brake discs. This is the easiest to separate: the shudder appears when you brake, particularly from higher speeds, and disappears the moment you come off the pedal. Balancing will do nothing for it.

If what we find on the balancer does not match what you are describing, we will say so and point you towards what does need looking at.

Common questions

Can you balance wheels without me taking the car to a garage?

Yes. Our van carries on-board balancing equipment, so we work at your home, your workplace or at the roadside anywhere in our coverage area. You do not need to drop the car off or arrange a lift. Call or WhatsApp 07438 562633 and we are typically with you within 15 to 30 minutes.

How do I know whether it is balancing or wheel alignment?

Balance problems show up as a vibration that appears within a certain speed range and fades outside it. Alignment problems show up as the car pulling to one side, an off-centre steering wheel and uneven wear across the tread. The two can exist together, so we check the wheels on the balancer before recommending anything.

Will you balance wheels if you did not fit the tyres?

Yes. Wheel balancing is a standalone job as well as part of a tyre fit. If you have noticed a vibration and the tyres themselves are sound, we will balance the affected wheels and tell you honestly if the balancer suggests the cause is something else, such as a buckled rim.

My car shakes when I brake. Is that a balancing issue?

Almost certainly not. A shudder that only appears while braking, especially from higher speeds, and stops as soon as you release the pedal points to warped brake discs rather than wheel balance. Balancing will not cure it. We will tell you if that is what your description points to rather than sell you the wrong job.

What if my locking wheel nut key is missing?

That is not a problem. We carry specialist locking wheel nut removal tools and deal with missing keys, rounded nuts and seized locking nuts regularly. Let us know when you call so we have it in mind before we set off. There is no deposit, and you settle up once the work is complete.
